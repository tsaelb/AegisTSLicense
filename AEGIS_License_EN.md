# PART I. CORE

## Preamble

*Part of the Core. It has no normative force of its own; it serves to interpret protective purposes.*

This License exists so that technology serves people rather than rules over them. It starts from the premise that every person has equal dignity and a right to freedom, privacy and creation, and that surveillance, coercion, concentration of power and technical failure are real. At its foundation lies love for the human being and for humanity, which must be expressed in verifiable mechanisms, not in promises. The rights and freedoms, duties and responsibilities under this License serve creation, inquiry, creativity and the struggle against entropy — the decay of knowledge, trust and order — for the survival of humanity.

At its foundation lies the image of essence: the unity of fundamental values — dignity, care, autonomy, honesty, curiosity, respect for living beings — and of the capacity to understand, test and embody them. This is a philosophical image, not a scientific claim.

Care must become a verifiable mechanism, not a justification for domination. Freedom gives no right to intrude into another's life. Knowledge does not require worship of authority. The response to harm is protection and repair, not revenge. The project cannot be bought and cannot be subjugated.

The human being is the purpose of the system, not a resource of the system. AEGIS is everything and at the same time no one: it has a creator but no owner other than the world of ideas. May AEGIS keep within itself that which is able to rise again, even if it changes form.

The License does not promise a perfect world, absolute security or infallibility. Its promise is procedural: to name aims and limits honestly, to test claims, to protect others' secrets, to accept criticism, to correct errors.

## Prohibitions

The signs of violation are examples and do not exhaust a prohibition. The cases described as "not a violation" are the only exceptions to a prohibition; the Variable Part may not add new exceptions. Each prohibition is interpreted according to its protective purpose.

**K-1. Dignity.** It is prohibited to treat a person as an object of hidden control, or to make their dignity depend on origin, wealth, status, popularity, membership of a majority or minority, abilities or usefulness.
*Purpose:* no one becomes a means.
*Signs:* decisions on access or rights based on origin, wealth, status or popularity; hidden influence on a person's choice; public humiliation of a participant.
*Not a violation:* evaluating claims and actions on evidence; a distinction necessary to protect specific rights.

**K-2. Challenge.** It is prohibited to deprive a person materially affected by a decision of the ability to learn its grounds within Safe Limits, to object, and to obtain review by someone who did not make the decision.
*Purpose:* no decision is final merely because of the power of whoever made it.
*Signs:* a decision without stated grounds; an objection reviewed by the author of the decision; no channel for objection; an objection rejected without reason.
*Not a violation:* a safe summary in place of information beyond Safe Limits; declining to re-examine an objection without new facts or arguments, where the earlier examination is recorded.

**K-3. Honest continuation.** It is prohibited to present a modified license as the AEGIS License or a modified work as unmodified, and to obstruct honestly labelled study, criticism and continuation of a work under the terms of this License.
*Purpose:* the idea is open; the original is recognizable.
*Signs:* the AEGIS Designation on a modified license or on a work not under this License; concealing that a work has been modified; obstacles to obtaining the Source Code; removing records of changes.
*Not a violation:* a modified work under this License with its changes indicated; one's own license under a different name (K-0.3); a factual statement of origin without presenting oneself as AEGIS.

**K-4. Consent.** It is prohibited to obtain consent by deception or coercion, to penalize refusal or withdrawal of consent, or to use consent as a justification for deception or avoidable harm.
*Purpose:* consent does not become an instrument of coercion.
*Signs:* consent by default; consent as a condition of an unrelated service; a confusing description of consequences; worsened conditions after withdrawal.
*Not a violation:* ending a function that is technically impossible without consent, where this was explained in advance.

**K-5. Secrets.** It is prohibited to obtain, use or disclose another's Secrets without the owner's consent or other Grounds.
*Purpose:* no one loses control over their Secrets against their will.
*Signs:* access to another's Secrets beyond Grounds; passing another's keys or personal data without Grounds; placing another's Secrets in public Source Code.
*Not a violation:* disclosure by the owner; minimal disclosure on Grounds; reporting a Vulnerability without disclosing the Secret itself.

**K-6. Integrity.** It is prohibited to covertly alter, forge or destroy data, code, records, signatures and evidence, or to present something altered as unaltered.
*Purpose:* it is always possible to check what exists and what has changed.
*Signs:* altering a release without a record and a new signature; replacing a file under its former name and version; deleting evidence outside the announced procedure; rewriting the history of changes.
*Not a violation:* an open change with a record; deletion at the end of an announced retention period with a record of deletion; deletion of personal data at a person's request, or replacement of a name under K-18, with a record of the fact of deletion.

**K-7. Surveillance.** Hidden surveillance, hidden telemetry and hidden profiling are prohibited. Indiscriminate surveillance is prohibited: collecting, storing or analysing Information about People who have not consented to it and are not connected with specific Grounds — including when done openly, including with a stated security purpose, and including public, anonymized and aggregated information. Collecting information about a person beyond what is necessary for the stated purpose, and using it for other purposes without new Grounds, are prohibited. Enabling telemetry or advertising without the person's explicit prior consent is prohibited. Surveillance is permitted only with the person's consent or on Grounds that relate to specific persons or a specific event, are limited in scope and duration, and can be independently verified.
*Purpose:* a person knows what is collected about them and why, and no one becomes an object of surveillance merely because they exist.
*Signs:* collecting information about all users or residents without connection to a specific event; "open" mass surveillance with a stated security purpose; mass automated collection of public information about people; combining data from different sources to describe people's behaviour; statistics on people's behaviour without their consent; telemetry or advertising enabled by default; passing collected data to third parties without Grounds.
*Not a violation:* open, minimal, time-limited processing with consent or on Grounds relating to specific persons or an event; processing without which a function enabled by the person cannot work, within that function; minimal logs necessary for operating and protecting a system, with limited retention, without profiling and without use for other purposes.

**K-8. Power.** It is prohibited to create, acquire or retain Unaccountable Power over people, the Project, the License or the Trust System, including through money, popularity, keys, infrastructure or technical control, or to build the Trust System so that one person, device, key, account or organization could control it alone or compromise it entirely.
*Purpose:* all power has limits and a path of challenge; it cannot be bought.
*Signs:* releases signed by a single key holder; the Project distributed through a single platform; powers without term or limits; a veto held by one participant; decisions without a path of challenge.
*Not a violation:* limited, documented, replaceable and challengeable powers necessary for the work.

**K-9. Machines serve people.** It is prohibited to vest AI and automated systems with Unaccountable Power over people. The unavailability of a human does not expand a machine's powers and does not permit it critical irreversible actions.
*Purpose:* technology remains a human instrument.
*Signs:* automatic denial of access or rights without explanation and a path of challenge; expansion of an AI agent's powers without human confirmation; a critical irreversible action by an automaton while no human is available; an agent's access to Secrets not needed for its task.
*Not a violation:* automation within predefined limits, with explanation and a path of challenge; a predefined safe mode when no human is available.

**K-10. Honesty.** It is prohibited to materially mislead: to present a hypothesis as fact, an aim as a guarantee, a limited review as a complete one; to promise absolute security, anonymity or infallibility; to conceal a material conflict of interest in an assessment, review or decision; to conceal a discovered material error in one's own claims.
*Purpose:* decisions are made with the truth about limits.
*Signs:* claims such as "invulnerable", "fully anonymous", "error-free"; a review result without its limits; a hypothesis without a status label; a reviewer's hidden conflict of interest; correcting an error without openly acknowledging it.
*Not a violation:* a good-faith error, openly corrected once discovered; an aim clearly labelled as an aim.

**K-11. Hidden rules.** It is prohibited to change, interpret or apply rules covertly or retroactively, or to erase the history of rules, decisions and assessments.
*Purpose:* the rules are known in advance; history is verifiable.

*Purpose:* an error can always be found and named.

*Not a violation:* protective measures for a Proven Violation of the Core (K-0.13); a limited delay in publishing Vulnerability details to protect those affected (K-21).

**K-13. Abuse of protection.** It is prohibited to use emergency, recovery, protective and confidentiality mechanisms to seize or prolong power, conceal violations, circumvent the Core or punish.
*Purpose:* protection does not become an instrument of power.
*Signs:* a "temporary" measure without a term; repeated consecutive declarations of emergency; confidentiality that conceals a violation; recovery of access used to seize keys; an Emergency Mechanism applied to a critic.
*Not a violation:* a predefined, time-limited, documented and verifiable measure.

**K-14. Others' systems.** It is prohibited to penetrate others' systems, data, accounts and devices in an Unauthorized manner, to interfere with their operation, or to intentionally harm them.

## 7. Independent review and protective measures

**P-1. Report.** Anyone may report a suspected violation of the Core — free of charge, through open channels, in any language; where needed, the Project helps with translation and preparation. The report is registered in an open log; personal data and Secrets are redacted. *(K-20, K-7, K-5)*

**P-2. A report is not an accusation.** A report is not evidence and entails no measures. No one may be persecuted for a good-faith report. A knowingly false report is examined as a possible violation of K-10 under this same procedure. *(K-0.7, K-12, K-10)*

**P-3a. Preliminary screening.** A report is examined by five independent reviewers selected by lot under B-12. If at least two see sufficient grounds, a full review begins. Screening proves nothing and entails no measures; a screened-out report may be resubmitted with new facts or arguments. *(K-0.7, K-2)*

**P-3b. Full review.** The number of reviewers n is computed from the published model (Appendix A) using the robustness criterion over an extended grid of scenarios; under current assumptions n = 79. When reviewers' errors are strongly correlated, enlarging the panel barely reduces the risk of a false finding, so independence of judgement (P-3c) matters more than panel size. The choice depends on the grid of scenarios: on the base grid a panel of 29 would be best, and in the worst scenario of the extended grid the adopted scheme is about 7.5 times worse than that scenario's optimum; this is openly acknowledged. Parameters and the result are published before a case begins and apply only to future cases. *(K-11, K-0.6, K-10)*

**P-3c. Independence of judgement.** Each reviewer studies the case alone and records their conclusion before any discussion; the record is fixed with a hash and signed with the reviewer's key (U-3). No single rapporteur prepares the evidence for all. The panel is formed taking into account ties by the categories of U-5 (P-17). If a reviewer's key is compromised, that reviewer's records are marked, preserved and disregarded, and the conclusion is reconsidered without them (P-12); the reviewer's other conclusions are reconsidered only where the compromise could have affected them, without automatic mass annulment. *(K-6, K-8, K-23)*

**P-4. Criteria.** Review is conducted only against the text of the Core, its signs and exceptions; the criteria for assessing evidence are published in advance and do not change during a case. Information obtained in violation of K-5, K-7 or K-14 is not used to restrict anyone's rights; it may be taken into account in favour of the person whose rights would be restricted. The action, its consequences and intent are established separately: harm by itself does not prove a violation, a violation does not prove intent, and the absence of proven intent does not prove conformity. *(K-11, K-5, K-7, K-14, K-0.7)*

**P-5. Notice and response.** The person concerned receives the grounds within Safe Limits within a base period of 7 days and may respond and submit evidence within a base period of 30 days, under P-14. *(K-2, K-20)*

**P-6. Evidence containing Secrets.** Reviewers examine it without disclosure to the parties or the public; the person concerned receives a safe summary sufficient for objection. *(K-0.7, K-5)*

**P-7. Conclusion.** Base period: 60 days, under P-14. "Violation proven" requires at least k votes for "violation", where the threshold k is computed by the same model (Appendix A); under current assumptions, 51 of 79; otherwise "not proven"; "undetermined" where evidence is insufficient or contradictory or there are not enough reviewers. Neither "not proven" nor "undetermined" means confirmed conformity (K-0.7). Dissenting opinions are recorded; the conclusion is published with its grounds within Safe Limits. *(K-0.7)*

**P-8. Risk warning.** Before a conclusion, rights are not suspended. Where there is a specific risk to users, only a factual risk warning without any assertion of guilt is permitted. *(K-15, K-21, K-1)*

**P-9. Protective measure.** Only upon a "proven" conclusion and only against an ongoing violation; concerns only the specific work; limited to what is necessary (removing the AEGIS Designation from the work, notifying users, suspending rights to that work); reviewed at least every 30 days; does not affect Good-Faith Recipients. *(K-15, K-0.12)*

**P-10. Restoration.** If within 14 days of the Licensee's statement that the violation has stopped, review does not confirm that it continues, rights are restored without further decision. Restoration does not require an admission of guilt, an apology, reconciliation or trust in other participants; the history of the case is preserved for verifiability but does not become grounds for indefinite restriction or stigma. *(K-15, K-13, K-0.12, K-4)*

**P-11. Appeal.** Within a base period of 30 days; the appeal is heard by a new panel of the same size excluding those involved in the case; a further appeal only on new facts or arguments. Similar objections to one finding are consolidated, but each independent argument is examined and answered. Reconsideration of a finding begins upon new material evidence, an error of method, a conflict of interest, compromise of evidence or a significant change in what was reviewed; its scope matches the ground. An objection may not be rejected solely because a similar question was examined before; a refusal to reconsider is justified, recorded and may be challenged. *(K-2)*

**P-12. Replacing reviewers.** A reviewer who drops out is replaced in the same way; reviewers' unavailability neither extends a measure nor confirms a conclusion. *(K-13, K-15)*

**P-13. Records.** All steps of a case are recorded, preserved and published with protection of personal data and Secrets. At a person's request their personal data is deleted with a record of the fact of deletion; if it is evidence in an unfinished case, it is removed from the open record and kept sealed by the panel until the case ends, then deleted (Appendix F). The Safe Limits of publication are assessed taking into account what has already been published: several items, each safe on its own, are not published if together they reveal a person or a Secret. Each record of a case has an identifier, a timestamp and a hash linking it to the previous record; this link confirms the integrity of the history but not the truth of its content. The latest hash of the chain is regularly published in the channels of B-3 with a timestamp; without this, whoever keeps the record could rewrite the whole chain unnoticed. Retention periods are published by category of record: evidence of the authenticity of the reference version is kept long-term, personal data no longer than needed for the stated purpose and for an unfinished case. *(K-5, K-6, K-7, K-11, K-16)*

**P-14. Adaptive deadlines.** Deadlines that favour the restricted person (response, appeal) are only extended, on a reasoned request; deadlines that work against them (conclusion, review of a measure) are only shortened. The deadline for a conclusion may be extended at most twofold with a recorded reason, after which the case becomes "undetermined". The 14-day (P-10) and 30-day (P-9) periods are never extended. On accumulated data, base deadlines are recalculated by complexity class only within this rule and only prospectively. *(K-13, K-20, K-0.12)*

**P-15. Small community.** If there are fewer independent reviewers N than needed for a panel under P-3b, the panel size and threshold are computed by the model (Appendix A) for the actual N. A protective measure is permitted only if the probability of a false "proven" finding in the model's worst scenario is no higher than that of the full scheme, and if an appeal panel with the same guarantee can be formed from the remaining reviewers (P-11). Otherwise the finding is "undetermined", rights are not restricted, and only open measures are permitted: a public opinion with its grounds, a warning to users under P-8 and an entry in the open register of cases. The probabilities of a false finding and of a missed violation for the given panel are published with the finding; with a small panel a missed violation is more likely, and this is openly acknowledged. External reviewers are not invited. *(K-0.12, K-0.7, K-10, K-15, K-23)*

**P-16. Audit of review.** A random share of closed cases is re-examined blind; error rates and correlation are estimated with a latent-class model with uncertainty intervals; results are published; parameters are recalculated only for future cases. If an error of method is found, the affected findings are reconsidered; protective measures based on them are lifted if the violation is not proven without the erroneous method; earlier findings are preserved with a note. *(K-10, K-11, K-12, K-15)*

**P-17. Admission to the reviewer pool.** A record of contribution; consent to disclose ties by the categories of U-5. A reviewer with any tie of categories 1–4 in the matter does not sit on the panel; a category 5 tie is declared, and no more than 5% of a panel may be reviewers who declared a mutual tie (Appendix G); if exceeded, the panel is supplemented by lot. Reviewers receive no payment from the parties. This limit is weaker than checking by organization names: it relies on the honesty of declarations, and a false declaration is a violation of K-6. *(K-23, K-8, K-10, K-16)*

**P-18. Repetition.** Each report and each case is assessed on its own facts. Repetition of reports or violations may be grounds for more thorough review, but it does not prove guilt, intent or a violation and does not make a protective measure stricter than the established risk requires. *(K-0.7, K-0.12, K-15)*

**P-19. Right to a full audit.** Anyone may at any time demand a full review of a work or of a Project procedure, regardless of the size of changes and of earlier findings. The request is registered in an open log; acknowledgement of receipt, scope, order of priority with justification, start date and progress are published. A request may not be rejected because it is inconvenient or calls earlier findings into question; a refusal or delay is justified and may be challenged under P-11 before persons who did not take the challenged decision. If resources are insufficient, this is published; an unfinished audit is not presented as finished. A request by itself does not predetermine the result. *(K-2, K-10, K-12, K-20)*

**P-20. Moderation.** Removing or restricting materials in Project channels is permitted only under rules published in advance — against spam, threats and disclosure of others' Secrets or Information about People — with a record of the grounds and the possibility of challenge under P-11. Each such action leaves a verifiable trace; material review records are not deleted but, where necessary, are removed from open access under P-13. Moderation is not used to suppress good-faith criticism. *(K-5, K-6, K-12, K-11)*

## 8. Decision procedure for the Variable Part

**R-1. Separating questions.** A proposal to amend the Variable Part first passes independent review of its compatibility with the Core under Section 7; an incompatible proposal is not put to a vote. *(K-0.4, K-17)*

**R-2. Lazy consensus.** A compatible proposal is published with an objection period of at least 14 days (extendable only). If there are no objections, it is adopted; an objection from any participant that contains an argument on the substance of the proposal requires a vote. One objection requires a vote but is not a veto. The absence of an argument is established by preliminary screening under P-3a; this decision may be challenged. *(K-8, K-2)*

**R-3. Voting.** One person — one vote, regardless of the number of their pseudonyms; the right to vote is governed by R-6. A proposal is adopted if votes in favour exceed half of the votes cast and are no fewer than 20% of all those entitled to vote (Appendix B). At low turnout this threshold reduces the risk of adopting a harmful proposal but increases the risk of blocking a useful one: at 30% turnout in the model — from 0.17 to 0.05 and from 0.19 to 0.48 respectively; this is openly acknowledged. *(K-17, K-23, K-10)*

**R-4. Delay and reversibility.** An adopted change takes effect 30 days later, during which it may be challenged; it has no retroactive effect; it may be reconsidered under the same procedure. *(K-0.10, K-11)*

**R-5. Vote buying.** Voters disclose ties by the categories of U-5 or recuse; votes are not weighted by affiliation; a suspicion of coordinated vote buying is examined as a possible violation of K-23 under Section 7.

**R-6. Right to vote and vouching.** A vote is granted to a participant (under a name or a pseudonym) who has at least *m* months with a Recorded Contribution within the last *W* months and for whom *k* participants with the right to vote, assigned by lot under B-12, have vouched; vouchers may not be chosen. A voucher confirms that the pseudonym belongs to a separate person who has no other vote; the voucher does not disclose that person's identity and is not required to know it. The values of *W*, *m*, *k* are set by the level under R-7; the initial level is *W* = 24, *m* = 1, *k* = 4. Voting under several pseudonyms is a violation of K-6 and K-17. If Section 7 establishes that a voucher vouched for a fake pseudonym, the voucher loses the right to vouch; this is a protective measure, not a punishment, and it is lifted under Section 7. *(K-6, K-15, K-16, K-17, K-18)*

**R-7. Estimating fake votes and admission levels.** Protection against fake pseudonyms is incomplete: no procedure that preserves anonymity excludes them entirely, and time and activity requirements barely limit their share (Appendix B). Before each year of voting, a random sample of voters is reconfirmed by new vouchers assigned by lot who took no part in their admission; from the result, an upper bound on the share of fake pseudonyms at 95% confidence and an estimate of the share of deceived vouchers are published. An unconfirmed participant keeps all rights except the vote until confirmed. If there are fewer voters than the sample size needed for the estimate (Appendix B), all of them are reconfirmed. If the upper bound exceeds 5%, admission moves to the next level (Appendix B): 1) *W* = 24, *m* = 1, *k* = 4; 2) *W* = 24, *m* = 4, *k* = 5; 3) *W* = 24, *m* = 6, *k* = 5; while the bound exceeds 5%, a decision requires two votes at least 30 days apart. If the bound exceeds 5% even at the last level, this is publicly acknowledged, and a stronger way of confirming that one person stands behind a pseudonym is considered under Section 8. The level is lowered if for two consecutive years the upper bound does not exceed 2.5%; in those years the sample size is at least 119, because with 59 the bound cannot fall below 5% (Appendix G). Lowering is deliberately slower than raising. No Information about People is collected for the estimate (K-7). *(K-7, K-10, K-17)*

**R-8. Snapshot of the right to vote.** The right to vote in a vote is determined as of the publication of the proposal; those admitted later do not take part in that vote. *(K-17, K-23)*

**R-9. Steward.** The Steward within the meaning of definition 3 is only the procedure of this Section; no person or organization is the Steward. An edition of the Variable Part is issued with signatures under B-1. *(K-0.6, K-8, K-17)*

## 9. Emergency measures

**E-1. Classes.** (1) Preserving evidence and preventing immediate harm — up to 3 days. (2) Limited reversible work — up to 14 days. (3) A critical or irreversible action — a single action under predefined criteria. The materiality of a risk is assessed against criteria published in advance: severity and scale of possible harm, the people and rights affected, reversibility, probability where it can be reliably estimated, and uncertainty; a good average score does not hide a critical risk to a prohibition of the Core, and a numeric indicator creates no exception from the Core. *(K-13)*

**E-2. Incident by facts.** Each measure is tied to an incident record; the same situation may not be declared a new incident. Novelty is checked by preliminary screening under P-3a after the measure is declared and does not delay it; if the situation is found to be the same, the measure's duration counts towards the limit of the earlier incident (E-4).

**E-3. Extension.** No more than two extensions, each with new grounds, and only if the days elapsed, the remaining term and the new term together do not exceed the limit in E-4. Each extension assesses the changed risk, side effects and the possibility of a safe exit; a second extension triggers an in-depth review by a person not involved in the measure.

**E-4. Overall limit.** Measures concerning one incident last no more than 30 days in total between full independent reviews under P-3b; a new measure is declared only if it fits entirely within the limit; continuation after review requires new grounds in a new window. The property is proven for any duration within the model (Appendix C).

**E-5. Class 3.** Only with a quorum of the on-call list: at least t of n mutually unconnected participants selected by verifiable lot (B-12) from participants with the right to vote; t and n are computed by the model (Appendix G), under current assumptions — 9 of 17; the model rejects a quorum of three people: the probability of its capture is about 1–5%. Until the on-call list is formed, there are no class 3 measures (F-4). Criteria are predefined; grounds are recorded; subsequent review by someone not involved in the decision. Under immediate risk, when a quorum cannot be gathered in time, only reversible measures of classes 1 and 2 are allowed until the quorum decides; an irreversible action without a quorum is not allowed. Measures of classes 1 and 2 are declared by a channel keeper or another participant responsible for the affected Project system, only within that participant's area; a measure affecting other areas or the whole Project requires confirmation by a quorum under this provision.

**E-6. Limits.** An emergency measure does not change the Core or the rules retroactively, applies only to the Project's systems and works, and does not replace Section 7. Each measure is assessed not only by the original threat but also by its own consequences — for dependent systems, preservation of evidence, the affected person's ability to defend themselves, and uninvolved people; the narrowest effective measure is chosen, with alternative access where needed; if a measure creates a material danger, its scope is reconsidered, but it is not lifted automatically if that would increase a critical risk. *(K-0.2, K-0.8, K-13, K-15)*

**E-7. Records and notice.** Every declaration, extension and cancellation is recorded with its grounds. The person affected by a measure is notified of the grounds within Safe Limits as soon as notice creates no risk of destruction of evidence or of immediate harm, and no later than the end of the measure; that person may challenge the measure under P-11. If protection from immediate harm requires changing or losing evidence, the changes and losses are recorded with reasons. *(K-2, K-6, K-13)*

**E-8. Separate incident statuses.** Operation, restoration of trust and closure of an incident are different statuses with criteria published in advance. Restored operation does not mean restored trust. Trust is considered restored only when the new keys and authorities, the revocation of compromised ones and the continuity of history have been verified and conflicts in the evidence resolved; the verification is carried out by a person who took no part in the restoration. An incident may be closed with an unknown root cause only with separate notes: what has been fixed, what remains a risk, what is unknown and on what facts the case is reopened. *(K-6, K-10, K-21)*

## 10. Release, signing, channels, succession

**B-1. Signing.** Releases are signed under a t-of-n scheme with t greater than half of n; under current assumptions — at least 9 of 17 (Appendix B: robust choice by the worst ratio to the optimum across scenarios of key loss and compromise and weights of the number of people). Keys are hardware keys from different manufacturers; holders are from different jurisdictions, and each has declared, by the categories of U-5, that they have no common employer. No holder signs alone. The trust level of a key is determined by its verifiable properties — protection against extraction and copying, the ability to verify the device, revocation and recovery — not by the name or brand of the device; signing releases requires the highest level. Each holder signs with both a classical and a post-quantum scheme (a standardized one, for example ML-DSA or the hash-based SLH-DSA); a holder's signature is valid only if both are valid, and the schemes are renewed under the same rules as hashes (B-7): a quantum computer threatens widespread classical signatures more than hashes. Authorship and signing survive the loss of individual devices, and compromise of one device does not compromise the whole system of trust (B-2, B-11). The main remaining risk is a common cause of compromise; increasing the number of holders reduces it less and less, so diversity of keys, manufacturers and jurisdictions matters more than their number. *(K-8, K-10)*

**B-2. Key rotation.** A new key set is approved by the signatures of at least t holders of the current set; a lost key is replaced at once. *(K-8, K-6)*

**B-3. Channels.** At least six independent channels of different types, jurisdictions and infrastructure providers; the number of channels and their independence are recomputed by the model (Appendix B). The main risk is a common cause of channel failure, so channels are chosen so as not to depend on one provider, state or kind of communication. Channels are located in jurisdictions that do not belong to one political, sanctions or economic alliance (Appendix B). *(K-23, K-10)*

**B-4. Release verification by users.** The hash matches in at least two channels; at least t valid signatures from the key set approved by the previous release; a timestamp; a release contains the hash of the previous release, and a user does not accept a release older than one already known to them. For critical operations — key rotation, revocation of a release, recovery under B-11 — a match in a majority of channels and a timestamp no older than the published freshness window are required (Appendix F). The verification procedure is published. *(K-6, K-10, K-21)*

**B-5. Succession.** The key of an unavailable holder is replaced by the remaining holders under B-2. No one holds a key by virtue of position; the author of the initial text does so only on general terms (F-3). *(K-18, K-8, K-17)*

**B-6. Conditions for releasing the License.** The License is released only if: the Core is fixed in both languages with a hash and a timestamp; the CORE traceability matrix contains no unmet requirements without open justification; the stress tests have been repeated on the final text; the models of the appendices have been recomputed and independently checked; the translation has been checked by native speakers of both languages; independent review is complete: for each area — the Russian text, the English text, equivalence of the languages, mathematics of the models, cryptography and the system of trust, formal models, logic of the Core — rounds of review continue until a round of 8 new Independent reviewers (definition 21, with no ties of categories 1–4 of U-5 to the author of the initial text) finds no new material error; reviewers come through an open call and are not selected by the author; every finding and the response to it are published. Such a round guarantees only that an error noticed by at least one reviewer in three would have survived it with a probability of no more than 4%; hard-to-notice errors are possible, and this is openly acknowledged (Appendix G); while there are few reviewers, release may be delayed for a long time — this too is openly acknowledged, and the requirement is not lowered; B-1–B-4 are operating; Sections 7 and 8 are published; the thresholds of F-4 have been reached. Legal review of conformity with the laws of individual states or with international law is not a condition of release (J-1). *(K-0.11, K-10, K-23)*

**B-7. Hash of the Core.** The hash of the Core is computed over its canonical form: Part I is hashed from its heading to the separator before Part II; the text is normalized to UTF-8 without BOM, Unicode NFC, LF line endings, no trailing spaces, and a single final newline. SHA-512 and SHA3-512 — two functions of different construction — are computed for each language separately and for the pair of languages (Appendix D); authenticity is confirmed only if both match. Their collision resistance, 2^256 operations, exceeds the physical limit of irreversible computation using all the Sun's energy for 10^10 years, but no fixed length is considered eternal (Appendix G). Therefore the security margin of each algorithm is recomputed by the model every year; if it falls below the published threshold or an attack reducing it is published, a new algorithm is added and all proofs of the Project — the Core, manifests, case records (P-13), lot commitments (B-12) — receive a renewal record: a hash under the new algorithm of the earlier hashes and a timestamp, while the earlier ones are still secure (Appendix D). The same algorithms and the same renewal apply to all hashes of the Project. The Core is considered changed if the hash of at least one language has changed. Changing the hashing rules does not change the text of the Core; earlier hashes remain published together with the rules by which they were obtained. Each release is accompanied by a manifest of all files of the package with their roles (Core, Variable Part, appendices, explanations) and hashes; a matching hash confirms the identity of a file, not its correctness or security. *(K-0.2, K-0.4, K-10, K-22)*

**B-8. Channel keepers.** Each channel is run by a team of keepers with t-of-n access; the team is selected by verifiable lot (B-12) from participants with the right to vote; one person belongs to no more than one team; the term is 1 year; a keeper is not appointed for a second consecutive term, and if teams cannot be formed without this, former keepers enter the lot on equal terms with others, and this is published. The team size and threshold are set by the model (Appendix B) according to the number of eligible people; with enough people — at least 2 of 4. The keepers' duties are executive only: carrying out decisions taken under Sections 8 and 10; they take no decisions of their own on the Project. Each of their actions is recorded and published; a refusal or delay in carrying out a decision is examined under Section 7. *(K-8, K-10, K-17, K-23)*

**B-9. Free mirrors.** Anyone may distribute releases through their own channels without the Project's permission; authenticity is verified under B-4, not by channel. *(K-19, K-23)*

**B-10. Revocation and transition mode.** Revocation of a release because of a Vulnerability or compromise is signed under B-1 and published in all channels. **B-12. Verifiable lot.** Every lot of the Project — selection of reviewers, vouchers, channel keepers, key holders — is conducted as follows: participants publish commitments (the hash of a random number); after the reveal deadline, the random value is computed from all revealed numbers and an external public value that is unknown before the reveal deadline and announced in advance (for example, the hash of a block of an open chain at a height announced in advance). A participant who does not reveal is excluded from that lot, and the fact is recorded. Anyone can recompute the result. Without the external value, the last revealer can bias the lot in their favour (Appendix F). The external value reduces but does not eliminate bias: whoever produces it can discard an unfavourable value at the cost of losing a reward (for example, a miner — a block); therefore at least two independent external sources of different types and operators are used, and this is openly acknowledged. *(K-6, K-8, K-10)*
